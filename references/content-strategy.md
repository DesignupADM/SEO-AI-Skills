# Content strategy and measurement

Use for keyword or prompt planning, content inventories, refresh decisions, or measurement requests. For a narrow rewrite, use only the relevant section. This synthesis draws on the five supplied articles documented in [source-review.md](source-review.md); it does not require their commercial tools.

## Map audience questions to page intent

Start with the business outcome, audience, language, market, and existing pages. Use available Search Console queries, customer questions, site search, keyword exports, and observed search results. Label invented example prompts as hypotheses, not measured demand.

Group related queries by user need: informational, navigational, commercial investigation, or transactional. Map each intent cluster to an existing page or a justified new page. Synonyms do not automatically need separate URLs. Inspect competing results to understand format and unmet needs, not to copy their wording or replicate every heading.

A compact planning record can include:

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Audience question or query cluster | Actual language used, source, and locale |
| Intent and desired action | What the visitor needs and the relevant next step |
| Existing target | Verified URL, or clearly labeled proposed page |
| Evidence gap | Missing answer, comparison, example, or proof |
| Priority | Business relevance, observed opportunity, effort, and confidence |
| Measurement | Available baseline and success metric |

Search volume, keyword difficulty, and AI prompt estimates require a named data source and date. Vendor difficulty scores are not interchangeable. Do not transfer search-volume numbers to AI prompts or fabricate metrics when tools are absent.

## Build a useful brief

Specify the audience, primary intent, page promise, necessary questions, distinctive evidence, relevant internal destinations, and conversion action. Choose title, headings, and supporting terms naturally; do not enforce keyword density or mandatory repetition in every section.

Identify an information-gain contribution the business can actually supply: a real example, original measurement, documented process, comparison criteria, expert explanation, or demonstration. Keep requests for missing evidence separate from publishable copy. Use question headings, lists, tables, or answer summaries when they improve the reader's task.

For new URLs, prefer readable, stable slugs. Do not rename a working URL merely to remove a number, date, or stop word. Existing URL changes require an explicit migration decision, redirect plan, and link/canonical checks within authorized scope.

## Inventory and refresh

For each in-scope page, record intent, owner if known, last substantive review if known, important claims, links, and available performance. Choose retain, refresh, investigate consolidation, or propose retirement based on usefulness and evidence. Age alone is not a defect; multiple pages mentioning the same keyword do not establish cannibalization.

Prioritize consequential inaccuracies, broken journeys, outdated product details, changed customer questions, and valuable pages with demonstrated declines. Check facts and sources, screenshots, link destinations, metadata, and next steps. Update dates only when the underlying content warrants it; do not promise a ranking benefit from cosmetic freshness.

Before proposing consolidation, compare the pages' intent, unique value, query overlap, links, and conversions where data exists. Do not delete, redirect, or canonicalize distinct useful pages as a routine content optimization.

## Reach and local relevance

Recommend useful, attributable resources that others may reference: original research, practical tools, examples, or explainers. Treat outreach, community participation, brand mentions, and content repurposing as optional distribution work with separate authorization for posting or messaging. Do not claim unlinked mentions guarantee rankings or AI citations.

Choose channels from audience evidence rather than a universal platform checklist. Local work depends on genuine locations, service areas, eligibility, and customer needs; small business size alone does not establish local intent. Verify business details and profile consistency when relevant. Shipping nationally does not rule out a separate local audience. Do not invent locations, reviews, or near-me phrasing.

## Measure business outcomes

Before changes, record the available baseline and reporting window. Separate:

- Visibility: impressions and observed query positions from a specified source.
- Visits: organic clicks or sessions, with channel and attribution definitions.
- Outcomes: qualified inquiries, purchases, or another agreed conversion.
- AI observations: exact prompt, engine/model when available, date, locale, cited URLs, and repeat observations. A sampled response is not population-wide visibility.

For CTR work, inspect query mix, device, country, position, seasonality, and result appearance before attributing a change to metadata. Search engines may choose different titles and snippets. Call an uncontrolled before/after comparison an observation, not an A/B test or causal result.

Assess costs and attributable outcomes only when supplied. If reporting ROI, define whether the numerator uses revenue or profit and which costs and attribution assumptions apply. Do not claim incremental profit from a revenue-only calculation or calculate ROI with zero/missing costs.

For performance issues, use available field or lab evidence and identify which it is. Pass concrete findings to engineering; do not prescribe dedicated hosting, a CDN, AMP, or a fixed load-time target from copy inspection alone. Search, indexing, and conversion results need ongoing measurement; no universal timeline is promised.
